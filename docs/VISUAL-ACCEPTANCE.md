### Which states were stubbed, and with what

The tracking answers and the registration success come from **`page.route()` stubs**,
not from a live server. This is deliberate:

* the tracking provider is a **third party we do not control**;
* `KHAIBAO9610_MODE` is **`mock`** — the real registration contract has not been
  supplied (issue #4), so no real customer code can be produced;
* a capture of a "success" screen that depends on a live upstream is not a capture of
  a success screen on a bad day.

The stubs speak the **documented contract**, copied verbatim from
`tests/e2e/tracking.spec.js` so the two cannot drift:

| Stub | Path | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Tracking, FOUND | `**/api/tracking/warehouse-imports/*` | `200` `{"result":"found","search_type":"warehouse_import","data":{…}}` |
| Tracking, NOT_FOUND | `**/api/tracking/warehouse-imports/*` | `404` `{"error":{"code":"NOT_FOUND","message":"Không tìm thấy mã vận đơn này."}}` |
| Registration success | `**/api/v1/registrations` | `201` `{"message":"Đăng ký thành công.","registration_status":"REGISTERED","external_customer_code":"TT99999",…}` |

Both tracking paths are always stubbed, so a capture can never quietly reach the real
backend through the half that was not stubbed. The successful captures were confirmed
by asserting the rendered state (`#trackingStatus` had class `is-found` / `is-notfound`;
`#formMessage` had class `is-success`) before the screenshot was taken — a state that
did not render would have failed the run, not produced a misleading image.

## 5. Items requiring human visual approval

**This is a list of things no automated check in this repository can decide.** Each
one needs a person to open the PNGs and judge. None of them has been judged.

1. **Alignment** — whether columns, headings, form fields and buttons line up on a
   shared edge across the desktop sections, and whether anything sits a few pixels off
   in a way that reads as sloppy rather than deliberate.
2. **Spacing rhythm** — whether the vertical gaps between sections feel even, or
   whether some sections feel cramped and others adrift. Automated checks measure
   thresholds, never rhythm.
3. **Whether the layout looks right** — that the page reads as a logistics business a
   customer would trust with their goods, in the intended order, with nothing important
   pushed below the fold or lost at a breakpoint. Look especially at the mobile
   registration screen (3308 px tall) and the mobile success screen (3638 px tall):
   long, tall pages are where "everything is on screen" and "the page is usable" stop
   being the same claim.
4. **Icon quality** — whether the brand mark, the menu icon and the favicon are crisp
   and consistent at both sizes, and whether any look like placeholders.
5. **Brand feel** — whether the red, the near-black and the type choices read as
   VIPORDER and not as a generic template.
6. **Whether the three colour changes actually look good** — "QUY TRÌNH" at 5.05:1 and
   the footer grey at 5.37:1 are **measurably accessible**; that says nothing about
   whether they are attractive. The lighter footer grey in particular was chosen to
   clear a contrast threshold — check that it does not now look washed out against the
   near-black, and that the lighter red on the dark section still reads as the same
   brand red used on white elsewhere on the page.
7. **The 12 px floor items** — the "QUY TRÌNH" eyebrow and the header tagline both sit
   exactly on the 12 px floor (see §4.1). Confirm they are legible on a real phone, and
   decide whether they should be larger.
8. **The 4.97:1 body copy** — the muted grey used for body text clears AA by ~10%.
   Confirm it reads as an intentional quiet grey rather than as text that is too light.
9. **Mobile menu open state** — whether the open panel
   (`mobile-412x900-04-header-menu-open.png`) covers what it should, sits where a thumb
   expects it, and looks finished rather than like a dropdown that fell open.
10. **The tracking states** — whether FOUND and NOT_FOUND are *visually distinguishable
    at a glance*, and whether NOT_FOUND reads as "check your code" rather than as
    "something broke". These were captured with stubbed data (§1), so the layout is
    real but the contents are fixed samples.
11. **The registration success state** — whether the confirmation and the "Đăng nhập /
    Kiểm tra đơn" button land with enough prominence that a customer knows they are
    done. Stubbed data.
12. **Dark section as a whole** — `#process` on near-black carries the eyebrow, a 50 px
    heading and body copy. Check the whole block as a composition, not the individual
    ratios.

## 6. What is NOT covered by any of this

Stated plainly so nothing here is mistaken for a clean bill of health:

* **Nobody has looked at the images.** This is the headline and it does not weaken.
* The accessibility suite audits the **homepage only**. `404.html` and every
  interaction-gated state (FOUND, NOT_FOUND, success, error, pending) were **not**
  contrast- or type-audited.
* Only **one** viewport per audit: the desktop suite runs at the Playwright
  `Desktop Chrome` profile (1280×720); `mobile.spec.js` runs at Pixel 7 (412×915).
  The **412×900** captures in this document are a third size that no automated audit
  covered.
* Horizontal overflow was checked at the **Pixel 7** profile only — not at 412×900 and
  not at any tablet width. There is no breakpoint sweep anywhere in this repository.
* No tablet breakpoint, no landscape orientation, no zoom/reflow at 200%, and no
  screen-reader run.
* These captures are of the **staging candidate**, not of a deployed host. Nothing has
  been deployed; there is no staging host.
* `docs/STAGING-RUNBOOK.md` §15 claims the containerised stack "publishes the whole
  repository as the web root". **That claim is stale** at this revision:
  `deploy/docker-compose.yml` mounts an explicit list of five public paths and does
  mount the security-headers snippet. The prose was written against `develop` @
  `8f83043` and was not updated when `deploy/docker-compose.yml` was fixed in
  `4445d60`. Flagged, not fixed here — it is outside this document's scope.
